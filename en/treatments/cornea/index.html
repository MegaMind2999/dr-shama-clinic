Corneal conditions and surgery

The cornea is the eye’s clear front window. When its shape or clarity changes, vision blurs, and the right treatment depends on why.

Why the cornea matters

The cornea provides most of the eye’s focusing power, so even small changes in its curvature or clarity can blur vision. Conditions that affect it include keratoconus, in which the cornea gradually thins and bulges into a cone; scarring after infection or injury; and changes in the corneal layers with age or after eye surgery.

Depending on the condition, treatment ranges from glasses or specialised contact lenses to procedures that strengthen or reshape the cornea, and transplantation when clarity is lost. An examination decides which applies.

Signs worth checking

  • Vision that keeps changing, or astigmatism that keeps increasing
  • Distorted or doubled images
  • Glare and sensitivity to light
  • Pain, redness or a feeling of something in the eye, which needs prompt review

At Dr. Shama’s clinic

Corneal surgery is one of Dr. Shama’s three stated areas of focus, alongside cataract and refractive surgery, and he is listed among the cornea specialists of i-Care Center for Ophthalmology in Smouha.

Questions worth asking at your consultation

  • What is causing the change in my cornea?
  • Is it stable, or is it progressing?
  • Which treatments fit this stage of my condition?
  • How often should my cornea be checked?
