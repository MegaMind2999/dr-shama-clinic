Cataract surgery and lens implants

When the eye’s natural lens clouds over, surgery replaces it with a clear artificial lens chosen for the way you live.

What a cataract is

Behind the coloured iris sits a lens that focuses light onto the retina. With age, and sometimes earlier after an injury, with diabetes or with certain medicines, this lens slowly clouds. That clouding is a cataract. Light scatters instead of focusing, so vision turns hazy, colours fade and lights glare.

Modern surgery removes the clouded lens through a very small incision, usually by breaking it up with ultrasound (phacoemulsification), and places a clear artificial lens, an intraocular lens or IOL, in its place.

Signs people notice

  • Cloudy or blurred vision that new glasses no longer fix
  • Glare or halos around lights, especially when driving at night
  • Colours that look faded or yellowed
  • Needing more light to read
  • Frequent changes of glasses prescription

Choosing your lens

Artificial lenses come in several designs. Monofocal lenses focus at one distance, usually far. Toric lenses also correct astigmatism. Multifocal and trifocal lenses aim to give near, middle and distance vision, though some people notice halos around lights with them.

Each design involves trade-offs, which is why the choice is made together, with your eyes, your work and your daily life in mind.

Questions worth asking at your consultation

  • Which lens designs suit my eyes?
  • Will I still need glasses for reading or for distance?
  • Should both eyes be treated, and when?
  • What should I expect in the days after surgery?
